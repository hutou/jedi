# Studying historical results

After daily use and the mechanics, this part examines what simulations show on past quotations. It assumes familiarity with scales, operations and the main capital questions.

A **search** compares scales over a period to choose one. A **backtest** simulates a fixed scale on historical quotations under explicit execution conventions. A **check over another period** examines that choice on data not used to tune it. **Dated monitoring** records decisions before observing their consequences.

These stages use Jedi commands, explicit dates and annotated reports. Chapter 21 prepares the question, securities, files and checks. Chapter 22 explains `tools backtest`, its assumptions and compact report. Chapter 23 follows a study on `Euronext_Growth`. Chapter 24 organises comparisons, interpretive limits and the results journal.

Reading distinguishes gross gain, fees, net gain, maximum investment and the valuation of remaining holdings. Daily quotations do not reveal the exact order of intraday movements; simulation rules must therefore be understood before commenting on results.

By the end, the reader will know how to prepare a historical examination, retain its definition and commands, compare scenarios and identify what needs further checking. A favourable past result remains an observation on specific data and assumptions.
