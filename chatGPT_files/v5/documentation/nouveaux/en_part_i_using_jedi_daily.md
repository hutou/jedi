# Using Jedi day to day

This part follows the user's path from an initial security idea to trade monitoring. It is for an investor who wants to understand the decisions and apply regular rules without first learning Jedi's formulas.

A **security** is the instrument purchased and sold. A **scale** defines the mechanics' prices and quantities. A **trade** brings together that management, its settings and operations. These concepts are introduced before their details.

Security selection can come from personal research, an external screener or `profile`. `score` then helps search for a scale over the chosen history; `candidate` evaluates opening with the available price and resources. The user keeps the choices and executes orders with their broker.

Chapter 01 presents the path; chapter 02 prepares installation. Chapters 03 to 06 move from security selection to proposal evaluation. Chapters 07 to 09 cover opening, monitoring and adaptations, including a journal of exceptional events. Chapter 10 explains quotation files and market databases.

Examples use Jedi commands and clearly identified data. Demonstration files are fictional: they explain outputs. After this part, the reader will know where to find useful information, what to record and what to check before proceeding.

Part II explains the mechanics behind these commands. Part III shows how to examine results using historical data.
