# The philosophy of Jedi

## General principle

Jedi is a method for managing a trade mechanically, based on predefined rules.

Once a trade has been opened and configured, the software determines the operations to consider according to:

- the scale in use;
- the current position of the trade;
- the operations already completed;
- any capital management rules that have been enabled.

Jedi does not try to predict the market's next movement to decide each day whether to buy or sell.

Its principle is to define rules for intervening in advance, then apply them consistently when the planned levels are reached.

## Working with price fluctuations

Prices do not generally move continuously in a single direction. Even within a lasting trend, upward and downward movements follow one another.

Jedi seeks to use some of these fluctuations through successive purchases and sales.

For example, a round trip can involve selling a quantity of shares after a rise, then buying it back at a lower price.

The price difference on that round trip is positive. Its net result nevertheless depends on fees, any applicable tax and the actual execution conditions.

The method therefore does not require the user to predict the security's future trend precisely.

It still involves risk: a prolonged adverse movement, insufficient liquidity, large price gaps or insufficient capital can impair or prevent the expected functioning of the trade.

## Decisions that remain with the user

Jedi automates calculations and applies rules, while the user retains responsibility for key decisions:

- choosing the security to examine;
- deciding whether to consider a new trade;
- making the final choice among the solutions proposed by the analysis tools;
- actually executing orders with their broker;
- deciding whether to continue or stop a trade when that decision is theirs to make.

Jedi's tools provide information and rules for managing a trade. They do not replace the investment decision.

## Reproducible rules

One of Jedi's central aims is to make operational decisions reproducible.

For the same situation and the same settings, the rules should lead to the same result.

This reproducibility also addresses a major human difficulty in trading: the tendency to decide under the influence of fear, impatience or euphoria. The rules are defined before the situation arises; Jedi then applies them without changing them in response to the emotion of the moment.

This property helps users:

- limit improvisation during the life of the trade;
- keep operational decisions from being dictated by emotions;
- maintain a precise record of decisions and operations;
- test the rules on historical data;
- compare different configurations;
- study their behaviour using reproducible methods.

The user keeps the responsibilities described above. Jedi separates those decisions from immediate reactions to market fluctuations and helps maintain the chosen discipline.

Reproducibility does not mean that future financial results are themselves predictable.

## What Jedi does not guarantee

Jedi does not guarantee:

- that a selected security will rise;
- that a scale will be profitable in every market situation;
- that a trade can always be funded;
- that a historical result will recur in the future;
- the absence of losses.

Statistical tools and backtests study how rules behave under defined conditions. They do not turn those observations into certainties about future results.
